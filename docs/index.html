Zacharias Frankel, Darkhe ha-Mishnah

The ways of the Mishnah, with an English translation paragraph by paragraph · דרכי המשנה

Zacharias Frankel (1801–1875), chief rabbi of Dresden and from 1854 the first head of the Jewish Theological Seminary at Breslau, published Darkhe ha-Mishnah (Leipzig 1859; Latin title Hodegetica in Mischnam) as the first critical history of the Mishnah and its sages. Its historical account of the Oral Law set off one of the great controversies of nineteenth-century Judaism. It has never been translated into English.

The Hebrew text

  • Base text: the first edition, Leipzig 1859. Scan: Harvard copy at HathiTrust, hvd.hwmg1g (public domain). The supplement (Tosafot u-mafteaḥ, 1867) is not included yet.
  • OCR: Kraken with the PP-OCRv6 model, line by line, then checked against the scan by eye. The print confuses similar letters (Frankel himself warns of ה and ח), so some words are read by sense; these are noted.

Method

  • Rows. One row is one heading or one paragraph of the print.
  • English. Close to the Hebrew; Frankel's rhymed prose in the preface is translated for sense, with biblical phrases identified in gray brackets.
  • Uncertain renderings have the mark ⚠ and a note at the end of the page.
  • Translator. The translation is by Claude (Anthropic), directed and reviewed by Ezra Brand.

Translations that exist

  • None in English is known.
